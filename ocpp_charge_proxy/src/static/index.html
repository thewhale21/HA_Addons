How it works
It's added to the schedule as a one-off slot (shown below, where you can change it) that unplugs at the first ready time your supplier accepts after these hours, and the ready time is set to it. If it meets a scheduled slot they become one. With Force schedule on supplier on, if it would take a day over the daily limit (Settings › Your supplier), you're asked first, and the next scheduled slot then starts just late enough to stay within it (and later ones too, if skipping it isn't enough).
Plugs in and unplugs at set times, so your supplier can charge then.
More
Each slot runs from a plug-in to its unplug, every week on the days you pick, or once on a date. Times are in your Home Assistant time zone. A time missed while the add-on is stopped isn't run later; if it starts inside a slot, it plugs in straight away. Unplugging during a session ends it. Two slots that meet (e.g. 14:00–15:30 and 15:30–18:00) carry on plugged in where they meet, as one charge with the ready time at the last one's end; they're still shown separately. Where your supplier's daily limit resets, they unplug and plug straight back in, so each is its own charge with its own ready time (Octopus: 06:00–12:00 and 12:00–18:00 are 6 hours before its 12:00 reset and 6 after). With Force schedule on, a slot longer than the daily limit across its reset time is split there the same way, as is a charge from auto plug-in, the car plugged in sensor or Charge now. Skip a slot to stop it running once. Turning the schedule on during a slot plugs in until its end, and a charge from auto plug-in, the car plugged in sensor or Charge now joins the slots it meets. Turning it off during a slot unplugs, unless one of those charges is going: that carries on to its own end. With the schedule off, those charges (and the charge before a saving session) still go in the schedule (in colour; your slots are greyed out). They stay separate from your slots, but still trim or skip them to keep within your supplier's daily limit, counted as if the schedule were on, so it all fits when it's turned back on (they join then). An off-peak time (add one the same way, choosing Off-peak time) keeps the schedule out of your off-peak hours (when the car may charge anyway): it unplugs at its start (in case the car is plugged in by hand), slots can't go in it, and auto plug-in, the car plugged in sensor and Charge now unplug for it and plug back in after, still ending when they would have.

Saving sessions

Reads your supplier's saving sessions from a Home Assistant calendar (or Axle Energy's sensors). What it does about them is up to the two switches below: move charging out of each session, and add a charge before it.
More
Octopus Energy's Saving Sessions (Power Down), E.ON Next's and EDF's sessions: any upcoming event in the calendar is a session. Axle Energy's events are read from its sensors (sensor.axle_energy_event_start, _end and _type), found automatically: its export events are saving sessions (its import events, when it wants you to use more, aren't). With both, sessions from either count. The calendar is read every 5 minutes, up to 8 days ahead. A session that's taken out of the calendar before it starts puts back anything it changed.

More

Octopus's Power Up and free electricity calendars are left out. If yours isn't found, pick it (tick "Show all sensors in the lists" on the Settings tab to see every calendar).
No charging during a session

More
Like an off-peak time, but only for that session: nothing plugs in during it, and auto plug-in, the car plugged in sensor and Charge now unplug for it and plug back in after, carrying on that much later so they keep their hours (3 hours from 17:55 with an 18:00–19:00 session: 17:55–18:00, then 19:00–22:00). A slot in the schedule that runs into a session keeps its end time (its ready time) and its hours, so it starts earlier by the time the session takes out of it (e.g. 15:30–21:30 with an 18:00–19:00 session: 14:30–18:00, then 19:00–21:30). If starting earlier would be in the past, it starts at the next half hour (if that's before its own start) and ends later by what's left (found at 14:40: 15:00–18:00, then 19:00–22:00); a slot already running ends later. Ending later stops at an off-peak time (the car can charge then anyway), and joins the next slot if it meets it. Shown in the schedule above, hatched.

Automatic schedule

Plans charging from your import and export rates: where exporting pays least, or before the high export times.
More
Each day (your supplier's limit day, e.g. 12:00 to 12:00 for Octopus, else midnight to midnight) whose export rates are known gets as many half hours of charging as you ask for, in the free half hours still to come where exporting pays least (not an off-peak time or a saving session). Your own slots give way where the rates are known (skipped, or shortened), and stay where they aren't yet (e.g. tomorrow morning before tomorrow's rates are out), and come back when it's turned off. Its charging is added to the schedule as one-offs (cyan), joined to a slot they meet. Auto plug-in, the car plugged in sensor, Charge now and saving sessions (the slots they move and the charge before them) come first: it plans around them, and they count against its half hours only where the day would otherwise go over your supplier's daily limit, which it always keeps to. Its own charging earlier in the day counts towards them. The cheapest import half hours (an hour or more together, e.g. 23:30–05:30) are off-peak times: hatched, and nothing automatic plugs in then. Planned again when the rates, the number of half hours, the daily limit or the rest of the schedule change, but not while one of its blocks is going. Rates: Octopus Energy's and EDF Energy's (today's and tomorrow's) are found automatically, else Predbat's (predbat.rates, predbat.rates_export), e.g. for E.ON Next; or pick any entity with a list of rates.

Plan

Populate schedule

Finds the best charging times from your house load's history and can make them your schedule.
More
Each day uses only that day's history from the previous weeks (e.g. Mondays from Mondays). The 30-minute slots with the highest average house load are picked per day; adjacent slots are merged. Use your house load power sensor (W or kW, not an energy sensor in kWh): it includes the car, so its highest is when you usually charged.

With a supplier's daily limit (Octopus: 6 hours, counted 12:00 to 12:00), each day runs from when the limit resets, so a night's charging counts as one day and the plan never goes over the limit. With no supplier, days run midnight to midnight.

Your off-peak times are kept: slots can't go in them, so busy half hours there move to the next busiest times, with a note.

The switch updates automatically: once a day from 12:00 (and straight away when it's turned on, or the sensor, weeks or slots change) the plan is worked out again and, if it's changed, made your schedule as Add to schedule does. It waits while a slot or charge is going.

Add to schedule replaces all your slots with the plan (off-peak times stay). The add-on's own charges (auto plug-in, Charge now, the car plugged in sensor) stay, and take the time they need from the new slots after them, as when they were planned.
